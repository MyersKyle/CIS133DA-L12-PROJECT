Web Users and Web Accessibility

Disability Description Common Barriers Accessibility Principles to Apply
Auditory Description: Partial or complete hearing loss that impacts the perception of spoken words or audio tracks. Common Barriers: Videos lacking captions or transcripts; audio content without text alternatives. POUR Principle: Perceivable — Provide synchronized captions, transcripts, and controls for background audio.
Cognitive and Neurological Description: Conditions affecting memory, attention, problem-solving, reading comprehension, or causing neurological sensitivities (like seizures). Common Barriers: Complex layouts, flashing animations, difficult-to-read fonts, and confusing navigation. POUR Principle: Understandable — Use clear, simple language, consistent navigation, and predictable functionality.
Physical Description: Conditions affecting mobility, motor skills, or dexterity, making standard mouse or keyboard usage difficult. Common Barriers: Lack of keyboard navigation support, small click targets, or requiring precise timed movements. POUR Principle: Operable — Ensure full keyboard accessibility, clear focus indicators, and sufficient target sizes.
Speech Description: Conditions or impairments affecting a person's ability to produce recognizable spoken language. Common Barriers: Relying solely on voice-activated controls or telephone-based customer service without text alternatives. POUR Principle: Operable / Understandable — Provide alternative communication methods like live chat, email support, or text-based options.
Visual Description: Blindness, low vision, or color blindness affecting a user's ability to read text or interpret visual content. Common Barriers: Low color contrast, missing alt text on images, unlabelled form fields, or non-scalable text. POUR Principle: Perceivable — Use high-contrast ratios, semantic HTML, proper alternative text for images, and scalable text.
